# Lithuanian learning programme

The programme uses a prerequisite sequence, explicit teaching, guided practice,
retrieval, and cumulative checkpoints. It is inspired by these primary sources:

- [Vytautas Magnus University A1 syllabus](https://www.vdu.lt/erasmus-studies/lithuanian-as-a-foreign-language-a1-2/): pronunciation and introductions before family, everyday life, food, home, and city tasks; connect grammar with communication.
- [Council of Europe action-oriented approach](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/action-orientation-in-the-classroom): organise learning around things a learner can do in real situations.
- [European School Education Platform classroom guidance](https://school-education.ec.europa.eu/en/discover/news/step-step-using-cefr-classroom): set learning objectives and use evidence of progress.

These sources inform the design; they have not accredited or reviewed the app.

## Sequence and routine

The 36 modules retain their stable identifiers but are ordered by prerequisite,
rather than the months embedded in the original identifiers. Foundations move
through sounds, greetings, introductions, people, questions, present-tense routines,
food, and shopping. Later modules introduce past and future forms before tasks
that require them. The programme provides A1 foundations and later A2 practice,
not a certified proficiency assessment.

Each module has six lessons: Learn, Forms, Listen, Conversation, Review, and
Checkpoint. Introductory lessons open directly on a question. Each exercise teaches its phrase
(or matching pair) beside the answer controls, with an optional language-pattern
reference. Learners can use this support while answering; independent recall comes
in later lessons. Incorrect matching explains the meaning before another attempt.
Review and checkpoints bring back material from earlier modules. Checkpoints
require at least two thirds of the scored exercises correct; missing or skipped
responses cannot earn credit. Speaking repetition is explicitly unscored.
Listening and conversation have authored activities when generation is unavailable.
Audio playback depends on configured Lithuanian speech support.

## Existing learners

Completed lesson IDs remain valid. In-progress attempts from the previous exercise
format restart safely; current-format attempts restore both answers and feedback.
Only phrases introduced by a lesson enter the Lithuanian Library. Records include
the translation, sentence example, language, and lesson source. Replays and review
lessons do not add duplicates. New phrase-based IDs cannot overwrite an unrelated
word saved under the old position-based identifiers.

Answered exercises count towards the active course's daily goal and local-calendar
streak; reading a guide or skipping an exercise does not. Historical lesson answers
are recovered once when possible. Course activity stays separate during backup
merges, and streaks are derived from recorded study days rather than an incrementing
counter. Practice records demonstrate activity, not an official language level.
